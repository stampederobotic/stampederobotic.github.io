About

The joint is where simulation meets the floor.

A policy trained in simulation assumes a clean joint: torque in, motion out. The real joint has a gear with play in it, friction, latency and compliance. Stampede builds actuators that close that gap from both sides: we take the backlash out in hardware, and we measure what remains on every unit and ship it as the model.

01

Near-zero backlash

Under 3 arcmin of play at the output, at the price of a standard planetary joint. Backdrivable, with an absolute encoder on both sides of the gear.

02

Measured, then shipped with its model

Our end-of-line rig records friction, torque constant, thermal response and latency for each serial number. MJCF, USD and URDF files come in the box. Train in MuJoCo, Isaac Lab or Genesis; deploy on the same unit.

03

Tested on a walking humanoid

Every design decision is checked on a humanoid test platform that walks and stands in our Lockport shop. The first customer to see a joint is us.

Factory

Our shop is at 210 Walnut Street, inside the Harrison Place incubator — the former Harrison Radiator works that built Lockport’s industrial century, twenty minutes from Niagara Falls. Machining, motor winding, assembly and the end-of-line measurement rig sit under one roof.

Western New York gave us the two things an actuator company needs most: people who have run production lines, and a region that still knows how to make precise things in metal.

Product

One joint, two sizes, the model included.

An integrated joint: outer-rotor brushless motor, two-stage reducer, cross-roller output bearing, dual absolute encoders and a CAN-FD driver in one housing. The small size is made for knees, shoulders and arms; the large size for hips and legs. Both come with the measured model for the unit in the box.

Community

Lockport builds robots.

Two of our founders met as teenagers on FIRST Robotics Team 1507, the Lockport Warlocks. The company came back to the same town.

We partner with Lockport High School and Team 1507: students come through the shop, drive our robot hands and arms, and see a hardware company being built twenty minutes from their classroom. If you teach or mentor in Niagara County and want to bring a group in, write to us.
